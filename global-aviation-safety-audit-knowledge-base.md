# Global Aviation Safety Audit & Incident Analysis Knowledge Base

This document compiles the core safety indicators, audit patterns, and repeated safety-critical vocabulary from leading global aviation oversight authorities, including IATA (IOSA), ICAO (USOAP), EASA, FAA, and DGCA India. It serves as an additional linguistic and contextual reference to optimize our Voice-to-Form Filling pipeline.

## 1. Safety Audit Frameworks & Methodology

| Oversight Entity | Program/Mechanism | Scope & Core Features |
|---|---|---|
| IATA (IOSA) | Operational Safety Audit | Transitioned in 2024 to a tailored, risk-based audit model. Distinguishes between Findings (non-conformity with an ISARP, requiring formal Corrective Action Plans) and Observations (advisory conditions for continuous improvement). |
| ICAO | USOAP CMA | Monitors State safety oversight systems against 8 Critical Elements (CEs) and Annex 19 (Safety Management). Average global Effective Implementation (EI) is 68.67% (2022-2024). |
| EASA | EPAS / SRM | Integrates incident findings into the European Plan for Aviation Safety (EPAS) via the Safety Risk Management (SRM) process, focusing on systemic, operational, and airworthiness safety. |
| DGCA India | GSC 01/2025 / eGCA | Implements multidisciplinary Comprehensive Special Audits and 360-degree ecosystem surveillance in response to critical accidents (e.g., Ahmedabad crash). Undergoing integration of a digital occurrence reporting platform on the eGCA portal. |

## 2. Incident Trends & Key Contributing Factors (Statistical Drivers)

Based on the latest curated multi-agency reviews (such as the IATA Annual Safety Report), the primary human and technical factors in commercial aviation accidents are:

- **Situational Awareness & Information Management (33%)**: The single most prevalent cognitive issue in flight deck and ramp operations.
- **Manual Handling & Flight Control (27%)**: 17% of all occurrences are specifically attributed to hand flying and approach path deviations.
- **Abnormal Runway Contact / Hard Landings (27%)**: Significant risk factor involving tail-strikes, heavy landings, or early touchdown.
- **Aircraft Malfunction / Technical Failure (23%)**: Mechanical, electrical, or systems losses contributing to flight anomalies.
- **Adverse Weather Conditions (20%)**: Windshear, gusty winds, and thunderstorms impacting flight control.
- **Non-Compliance to Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) (13%)**: Direct procedural deviations from established checklists.

## 3. High-Frequency "Repeated Words" and Safety Audit Lapses

Forensic analysis of surveillance reports (specifically from recent DGCA airport audits at IGI Delhi and CSMIA Mumbai) reveals repeated, highly specific phrases that indicate system vulnerabilities:

### Technical & Airworthiness Lapses
- **"Repetitive Defects" / "Recurring Faults"**: Reported technical snags that re-appear multiple times on an aircraft due to ineffective initial rectification or inadequate monitoring.
- **"Work Order Non-Compliance"**: Maintenance carried out without following the prescribed sequence of work or skipping standard checks.
- **"AMM Non-Adherence"**: Failing to take safety precautions prescribed in the Aircraft Maintenance Manual (AMM) (e.g., leaving a flap/slat lever or unserviceable thrust reverser system unlocked during maintenance).
- **"AME Absence"**: Aircraft Maintenance Engineers (AME) failing to physically attend to or supervise critical snag rectifications.
- **"Worn Tyres"**: Aircraft released or held up in service with tyres worn beyond limits.

### Ground & Airport Infrastructure Lapses
- **"Faded Runway Markings"**: Centerline or touchdown zone paint that has faded, presenting a low-visibility runway excursion risk.
- **"Speed Governor Deficiencies"**: Vehicles operating on the active ramp/airside area without functional speed limiters or speed governors.
- **"Unserviceable Ground Equipment"**: Defective ground handling assets, such as broken baggage trollies, left in active movement zones.
- **"Outdated Obstruction Surveys"**: Obstacle limitation charts that have not been updated despite new vertical structures being built nearby.
- **"Non-Unidirectional Center Lights"**: Airside taxiway lighting alignments that fail to conform to directional safety standards.
